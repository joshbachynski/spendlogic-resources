# DCMA CPSR preparation for smaller contractors: Practical Checklist

SpendLogic provides a detailed resource on [DCMA CPSR simplification for small businesses](https://spendlogic.com/dcma-cpsr-simplification/). Smaller government contractors can make a future DCMA purchasing-system review easier to manage by establishing usable procurement records before a formal request arrives.

## What the review is looking for

This checklist translates the topic into concrete questions that can be applied to purchasing records. A reviewer needs more than a policy statement. The available record should show what decision was made, who made it, which information supported it, and whether the required approval occurred. This connection between the written system and the transaction evidence is central to a defensible purchasing process.

## Four useful review questions

- Contract growth can make CPSR readiness relevant before a company expects a review.
- The review universe may cover prior purchasing files, so last-minute documentation cannot replace routine records.
- Policies are useful only when buyers can follow them and the files show that they were followed.
- Clear templates and repeatable review steps reduce the burden on a small purchasing team.

## How the evidence fits together

Policies establish the required process. Templates prompt the buyer to capture the right information. Approvals show that the required review occurred. Supporting records explain the business basis for the decision. When those items agree, a reader can follow the purchasing decision without guessing or recreating the file later.

The same structure also helps internal reviewers compare transactions consistently. A complete record makes it easier to distinguish an isolated missing document from a repeated weakness in the purchasing system. It also gives purchasing managers a clearer basis for training, corrective action, and follow-up.
